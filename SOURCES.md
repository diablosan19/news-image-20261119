## Lead — Trump, Xi and Putin hold first-ever trilateral summit in Shenzhen

- **News e Samay, 19 Nov 2026** ("Trump, Xi and Putin issue peace
  declaration, but where is the deal?"): the three leaders held their
  first-ever trilateral meeting in Shenzhen on Thursday 19 November, on the
  APEC sidelines; talks lasted about 80 minutes; afterwards China and Russia
  made public a broad "peace declaration" calling for UN reform, more refined
  petroleum supplies and reduced nuclear risks; the declaration does not
  mention Ukraine or Iran.
- **The Daily Jagran, 19 Nov 2026** (APEC summit report, 17:32 IST): the
  declaration was issued after the 90-minute meeting, the first-ever by the
  top leaders of the three major countries; the four points are UN reform,
  energy supply, the ICC and nuclear risk; "a nuclear war cannot be won and
  must never be fought".
- **statement.com, 19 Nov 2026, 14:13** ("Trump, Putin and Xi adopt peace
  pledge"): the leaders exchanged views on the war in Ukraine, the situation
  in the Middle East and on the Korean Peninsula, and on nuclear
  non-proliferation; before the trilateral, Trump met separately with Xi and
  with Putin; Trump then flew back to the United States.
- **Particle News, 19 Nov 2026** ("Trump, Xi and Putin Hold First Ever
  Trilateral Summit in Shenzhen"): the meeting took place at the Qilin Villa
  in Shenzhen for about an hour to 90 minutes; no binding arms-control deal
  was announced.
- **Wikisource, full text of the declaration** ("China-Russia-U.S. Heads of
  State Declaration toward Peace"): the four points verbatim, including the
  reaffirmation that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought.
- Page wording: "90 minutes" follows the Jagran/Particle figure (News e
  Samay says about 80); the agenda (Ukraine, Iran, nuclear arms) is framed
  as what was discussed, and the page states plainly that Ukraine and Iran
  were not named in the declaration text itself.

## Justice — ICJ finds Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

- **The Bangladesh Today, 19 Nov 2026** ("Myanmar guilty of genocide
  against Rohingya, says top UN court"): the ICJ said on Thursday that
  Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya minority, ordering
  refugees be given safe passage home and awarded compensation; the first
  time in the court's 80-year history that its judges have determined a state
  committed genocide; ICJ President Yuji Iwasawa delivered the verdict.
- **Fortify Rights, 19 Nov 2026** (The Hague): the ICJ found Myanmar
  responsible for committing genocide in *The Gambia v. Myanmar*, and
  concluded Myanmar had failed to hold the perpetrators accountable; "The
  Court is fully convinced that Myanmar committed acts under Article II (a),
  (b), and (d) of the Genocide Convention with an intent to destroy a
  substantial part of the Rohingya population of Myanmar"; the first time the
  ICJ has found a state responsible for committing genocide.
- **The Daily Star (TBS News), 19 Nov 2026** (sourcing BBC): the court
  found by nine votes to six that Myanmar had committed genocide; by the same
  margin it ruled Myanmar had violated its obligation to prevent genocide;
  the court estimated around 10,000 Rohingya were killed during the 2017
  clearance operations.
- Page wording: "By 9 votes to 6" and "first such ruling against a state"
  follow TBS/Fortify Rights; the 10,000-killed figure is in SOURCES.md only.

## Arctic — Denmark ratifies the Greenland security deal, 109–0

- **Reuters via 935 The Lloyd, 19 Nov 2026, 09:14 UTC** ("Denmark ratifies
  Trump deal over Greenland"): Denmark's parliament on Thursday unanimously
  ratified a security agreement with the US covering Greenland; backed by 109
  lawmakers, none against; the deal expands the US military's role, including
  permission to establish a "Golden Dome" missile defence system, while
  reaffirming Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders' right to
  self-determination.
- **AP via EuropeSays, 19 Nov 2026, 12:19 UTC** ("Greenland security:

  final step; the US can expand at Pituffik Space Base and set up defence
  areas at Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig; the deal was signed in September in
  New York after Trump repeatedly threatened to take control of the island.
- Page wording: "109–0" and "Golden Dome" follow both reports; "keeping
  Danish sovereignty" is the reaffirmation both outlets state.
